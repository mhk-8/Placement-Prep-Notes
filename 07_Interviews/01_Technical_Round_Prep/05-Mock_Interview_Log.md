
# Mock Interview Log

> **Why mocks matter more than solo practice.** Solving a problem alone tests knowledge. Solving it
> while a stranger watches, with a clock running, in an editor with no autocomplete, tests the
> thing you are actually being assessed on. Those are different skills, and only one of them
> improves by doing more LeetCode.
>
> **Copy the template block below into a new file per mock**, named
> `YYYY-MM-DD_<partner-or-platform>.md`.

---

## The single most valuable habit ⭐⭐⭐

**Be the interviewer as often as you are the candidate.**

Sitting on the other side teaches you things nothing else does: how obvious a candidate's silence
feels, how much a clear complexity statement reassures, how quickly you stop trusting someone who
guesses a number. Most candidates never do it, and it is free.

---

## How to run a mock properly

```
BEFORE
  □ Agree the format in advance: 45 minutes, one medium problem, or a rapid-fire round
  □ Use a plain shared editor with NO autocomplete and NO compiler
    (Google Docs, a bare CoderPad, or a text file on a shared screen)
  □ Camera on. It changes how you behave, and the real round will have one
  □ The interviewer picks a problem the candidate has NOT seen

DURING
  □ Full protocol every time — clarify, examples, brute force, optimise, code, test
  □ Interviewer: give a hint only after 3-4 minutes of genuine stuckness, and note WHEN
  □ Interviewer: write down long silences with timestamps ⭐ the most useful feedback there is
  □ No pausing, no looking anything up

AFTER
  □ 10 minutes of feedback, structured by the four axes (problem solving, coding,
    communication, verification)
  □ Candidate fills in this log within 30 minutes
  □ Re-solve the problem alone, properly, and add it to the mistake log
```

**What to look for ⭐:**
```
- Is the TOP FAILURE CAUSE column changing? If it has read "went silent" five times,
  more problems will not fix it — deliberate narration practice will.
- Is the communication score rising even when the problem is unsolved? That is real progress.
- Are you solving but never optimal? Work on pattern recognition, not on speed.
- Are you optimal but out of time? Work on speed, not on patterns.
```

## Recommended mock schedule

| Phase | Frequency | Type |
|---|---|---|
| **Build** (8+ weeks out) | 1 / week | Peer DSA, alternating roles |
| **Ramp** (4-8 weeks out) | 2 / week | 1 peer DSA + 1 project deep dive or CS fundamentals |
| **Peak** (1-4 weeks out) | 3 / week | Company-specific; at least one with a senior or an external platform ⭐ |
| **Taper** (final 3 days) | 0-1 | Light only. No hard mocks the day before ⚠️ |

⚠️ **Do not take a hard mock the day before a real interview.** A bad session damages confidence far
more than the practice helps. Re-read your project files and sleep.
