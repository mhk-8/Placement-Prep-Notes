
# Grammar and Error Spotting

> **Why it matters.** Verbal is typically 20-25% of a mass-recruiter OA, and error spotting,
> sentence correction and fill-in-the-blanks are the bulk of it. Unlike RC, this section is
> **pure rule recall** — the same fifteen rules generate almost every question ever asked.

---

## The error-spotting method ⭐⭐⭐

Questions give a sentence split into parts (A)(B)(C)(D) and ask which contains the error. Do
**not** read for meaning. Run this checklist:

```
1. Find the SUBJECT and the VERB. Do they agree in number?          ← ~30% of all errors
2. Check the TENSE. Is it consistent across the sentence?           ← ~15%
3. Check every PREPOSITION against its fixed collocation.           ← ~15%
4. Check PRONOUNS: number, case, and a clear antecedent.            ← ~10%
5. Check ARTICLES: a / an / the / none.                             ← ~10%
6. Check MODIFIER placement — is it next to what it modifies?       ← ~5%
7. Check PARALLELISM in any list or comparison.                     ← ~5%
8. Check COMPARATIVES and superlatives.                             ← ~5%
9. Only then consider word choice and redundancy.
```

⚠️ **The most important habit:** in "no error" questions, roughly 15-20% of items genuinely have
no error. Do not manufacture one. If nothing on the checklist fires, choose "no error".

## Rule 1 — Subject-verb agreement ⭐⭐⭐

**The core rule:** a singular subject takes a singular verb; a plural subject takes a plural verb.
The difficulty is always in **identifying the subject**.

| Situation | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Words between subject and verb | **Ignore them** ⭐ | *The **box** of chocolates **is** on the table.* (not *are*) |
| "along with / as well as / together with / in addition to / accompanied by" | Do **not** change the number ⭐⭐ | *The **manager**, along with his assistants, **is** attending.* |
| "either…or", "neither…nor", "not only…but also" | Verb agrees with the **nearer** subject | *Neither the players nor the **coach was** present.* |
| "Both…and" | Always plural | *Both the coach and the players **were** present.* |
| Collective nouns (team, jury, family, government) | Singular when acting as a unit; plural when members act individually | *The jury **has** reached a verdict.* / *The jury **were** divided.* |
| "Each, every, either, neither, everyone, someone, nobody, anybody" | Always **singular** ⭐⭐ | *Each of the students **has** a book.* *Everyone **is** ready.* |
| "None of" | Traditionally singular; plural is now accepted with plural nouns | *None of the work **is** done.* |
| "A number of" vs "The number of" | *A number of* = plural; *The number of* = singular ⭐ | *A number of students **are** absent.* / *The number of students **is** 40.* |
| Uncountable nouns (news, information, advice, furniture, luggage, equipment, machinery, poetry, scenery, staff*) | Singular; **no plural -s** ⚠️ | *The news **is** good.* Never *"informations"*, *"advices"*, *"furnitures"* |
| Nouns plural in form, singular in sense (mathematics, physics, economics, politics, news, measles) | Singular | *Mathematics **is** difficult.* |
| Plural-only nouns (scissors, trousers, spectacles, tongs, premises) | Plural | *The scissors **are** sharp.* |
| Amounts, distances, periods as a single unit | Singular ⭐ | *Twenty kilometres **is** a long walk.* *Five years **has** passed.* |
| "There is / There are" | Agrees with the **following** noun | *There **are** many reasons.* |
| Subject after the verb (inversion) | Find the real subject | *On the table **were** three books.* |
| Relative pronoun subject (who/which/that) | Agrees with its **antecedent** ⭐ | *He is one of those men who **are** always late.* |

⭐ **The "one of those X who" construction** is a perennial trap: the verb after *who* is **plural**
because *who* refers to *those X*, not to *one*.

## Rule 2 — Tense ⭐⭐⭐

### The twelve tenses

| | Simple | Continuous | Perfect | Perfect Continuous |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Past** | wrote | was writing | had written | had been writing |
| **Present** | write / writes | am/is/are writing | have/has written | have/has been writing |
| **Future** | will write | will be writing | will have written | will have been writing |

### The rules that are actually tested

```
⭐ SEQUENCE OF TENSES: if the main clause is past, the subordinate clause is usually past too.
    He said that he WAS ill.          (not "is")
    EXCEPTION: universal truths stay present.
    The teacher said that the earth REVOLVES around the sun.     ⭐⭐

⭐ SINCE / FOR  → present perfect or present perfect continuous, never simple present.
    He HAS BEEN working here since 2020.     (not "is working since")
    ⚠️ "since" takes a POINT in time (since Monday); "for" takes a DURATION (for two hours).

⭐ AFTER TIME CONJUNCTIONS (when, until, before, after, as soon as, by the time),
  use the PRESENT for future meaning:
    I will call you when I REACH home.       (not "will reach")   ⭐⭐

⭐ SECOND CONDITIONAL: If + past simple, would + base verb.
    If I WERE rich, I WOULD travel.          (were, not was — subjunctive)  ⭐
    THIRD CONDITIONAL: If + had + past participle, would have + past participle.
    If I HAD STUDIED, I WOULD HAVE PASSED.
    ⚠️ Never "If I would have studied" — the "would" never appears in the if-clause.

⭐ PAST PERFECT marks the EARLIER of two past actions.
    The train HAD LEFT before I reached the station.

⚠️ "had" + past participle, not past tense:  "had gone" not "had went".
⚠️ After "did", use the BASE form:  "Did he WENT?" ✗  →  "Did he GO?" ✓
```

## Rule 3 — Prepositions ⭐⭐⭐

There is no logic here; these are **fixed collocations to memorise**. They are among the most
frequently tested items.

| Verb/adjective | Preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| accused | **of** | accused of theft |
| charged | **with** | charged with murder |
| suspected | **of** | |
| afraid / scared | **of** | |
| agree **with** a person / agree **to** a proposal / agree **on** a matter | ⭐ three different ones | |
| angry **with** a person / angry **at** a thing | ⭐ | |
| comply | **with** | |
| composed | **of** | |
| congratulate someone | **on** | congratulate him on his success |
| consist | **of** | |
| capable | **of** | |
| depend / rely | **on** | |
| deprive | **of** | |
| different | **from** ⭐ (not "than" in formal use) | |
| die **of** a disease / die **from** an injury | ⭐ | |
| familiar **with** a thing / familiar **to** a person | ⭐ | |
| good / bad **at** a skill | (not "in") | good at mathematics |
| insist | **on** | |
| interfere **with** a thing / **in** a matter | | |
| jealous / envious | **of** | |
| married | **to** ⚠️ (not "with") | |
| object | **to** | |
| prefer X **to** Y ⚠️ (not "than") | ⭐⭐ | I prefer tea to coffee |
| proud | **of** | |
| responsible **for** a thing / **to** a person | | |
| similar | **to** | |
| superior / inferior / senior / junior **to** ⚠️ (never "than") | ⭐⭐ | He is senior to me |
| suffer | **from** | |
| sympathise | **with** | |
| tired **of** (bored) / tired **from** (exhausted) | | |
| wait | **for** | |
| worthy | **of** | |

⚠️ **Verbs that take NO preposition** (a very common planted error):
```
discuss, describe, enter, reach, resemble, marry, lack, comprise, accompany,
order, attack, investigate, resist, approach, emphasise, board, sign
  "Discuss ABOUT the matter"   ✗  →  "Discuss the matter"    ✓
  "Enter INTO the room"        ✗  →  "Enter the room"        ✓  (but "enter into an agreement" ✓)
  "Reach AT the station"       ✗  →  "Reach the station"     ✓
  "Emphasise ON the point"     ✗  →  "Emphasise the point"   ✓
  "He resembles WITH his father" ✗ → "He resembles his father" ✓
```

## Rule 4 — Articles ⭐⭐

```
A   before a CONSONANT SOUND:  a book, a university (/yu/), a one-rupee note (/w/), a European
AN  before a VOWEL SOUND:      an apple, an hour (silent h), an MBA (/em/), an honest man, an X-ray
```
⚠️ **Sound, not spelling.** *A university* but *an umbrella*; *an hour* but *a horse*; *an MP* but
*a manager*.

**THE is used:**
```
- for something already mentioned or uniquely identified   (the sun, the Earth, the Prime Minister)
- with superlatives                                         (the best, the tallest)
- with rivers, seas, oceans, mountain RANGES, deserts, groups of islands
    the Ganges, the Pacific, the Himalayas, the Sahara, the Maldives
- with nationalities and adjectives used as plural nouns     (the rich, the British)
- with ordinals                                              (the first, the second)
- with musical instruments                                   (play the guitar)
- with newspapers                                            (the Hindu)
```

**THE is NOT used:**
```
- before proper nouns, most countries, languages, meals, sports, academic subjects
    India, English, breakfast, cricket, physics
  ⚠️ EXCEPTIONS with "the": the USA, the UK, the Netherlands, the Philippines, the UAE
- before abstract and uncountable nouns used generally:  Honesty is the best policy
- before single mountains and lakes:  Mount Everest, Lake Victoria
- with "go to bed / school / church / hospital / prison" when the PURPOSE is meant ⭐
    He went to hospital (as a patient)  vs  He went to THE hospital (to visit someone)
```

## Rule 5 — Pronouns ⭐⭐

```
CASE:  Subject  I, we, he, she, they, who
       Object   me, us, him, her, them, whom

⭐ After a preposition, use the OBJECT form:  "between you and ME"  (never "between you and I")
⭐ In comparisons with "than/as", supply the missing verb to test:
      "He is taller than I (am)"  ✓        "He likes her more than me" vs "...more than I (do)"
        — the two mean different things; both can be correct.
⭐ WHO (subject) vs WHOM (object):  test by substituting he/him.
      "Who/whom did you meet?"  → "I met HIM"  → **whom**
      "Who/whom is at the door?" → "HE is"     → **who**
```

**Agreement and antecedents:**
```
⚠️ "Everyone should bring THEIR book"  — traditionally "his or her"; "their" is now widely
   accepted, but an OA answer key usually expects the singular. Prefer HIS OR HER in a test.
⚠️ A pronoun must have ONE clear antecedent:
   "When Ram met Shyam, HE was tired."   → ambiguous → rewrite.
⭐ Reflexive pronouns cannot be subjects:
   "Myself and my friend went"  ✗  →  "My friend and I went"  ✓
⭐ ONE … ONE'S, not one … his:  "One must do ONE'S duty."  ⭐ (a classic Indian-exam favourite)
```

## Rule 6 — Modifiers ⭐

**A modifier must sit next to what it modifies.**

```
DANGLING MODIFIER ⚠️
  ✗ "Walking down the street, the trees looked beautiful."   (the trees were walking?)
  ✓ "Walking down the street, I thought the trees looked beautiful."

MISPLACED MODIFIER
  ✗ "He almost drove for six hours."      (he almost drove — but didn't?)
  ✓ "He drove for almost six hours."

ONLY — the most movable word in English ⭐
  "Only he told her he loved her."   (nobody else told her)
  "He only told her he loved her."   (he told, but did nothing more)
  "He told only her he loved her."   (he told nobody else)
  "He told her he loved only her."   (he loves nobody else)
  Place "only" IMMEDIATELY BEFORE the word it limits.
```

## Rule 7 — Parallelism ⭐⭐

Items in a list or comparison must have the **same grammatical form**.

```
✗ "She likes swimming, cycling and to run."
✓ "She likes swimming, cycling and running."

✗ "He is honest, hardworking and a man of integrity."
✓ "He is honest, hardworking and full of integrity."

✗ "The job requires not only skill but also you must be patient."
✓ "The job requires not only skill but also patience."

⭐ Correlative pairs must bracket parallel structures:
   not only … but also        either … or        neither … nor
   both … and                 whether … or       between … and (never "between … to")
```

## Rule 8 — Comparatives and superlatives ⭐

```
Two things   → COMPARATIVE (-er / more)      "the taller OF THE TWO"
Three or more → SUPERLATIVE (-est / most)    "the tallest OF THE THREE"

⚠️ DOUBLE COMPARATIVE:  "more better" ✗, "most easiest" ✗
⚠️ ABSOLUTE adjectives cannot be compared: unique, perfect, complete, round, empty, final, supreme.
   "more unique" ✗ ;  "most perfect" ✗
⭐ Comparisons must be LOGICAL and LIKE-WITH-LIKE:
   ✗ "The climate of Chennai is hotter than Bengaluru."   (climate vs city)
   ✓ "The climate of Chennai is hotter than THAT OF Bengaluru."
⭐ "than any other" when comparing within the same group:
   ✓ "Delhi is larger than ANY OTHER city in the region."
   ✗ "Delhi is larger than any city in the region."   (it would have to be larger than itself)
⭐ Latin comparatives take TO, not THAN: superior, inferior, senior, junior, prior, anterior.
```

## Rule 9 — Conditionals and the subjunctive ⭐

```
Type 0 (general truth):  If you heat water, it boils.
Type 1 (real future):    If it rains, I will stay home.
Type 2 (unreal present): If I were you, I would accept.            ⭐ "were" for all persons
Type 3 (unreal past):    If he had studied, he would have passed.

SUBJUNCTIVE after these verbs — always the BASE form, no -s: ⭐⭐
   suggest, recommend, demand, insist, propose, request, order, require, essential, important
   ✓ "I suggest that he GO immediately."      (not "goes")
   ✓ "It is essential that she BE present."   (not "is")

⚠️ "Wish" takes the past for present regret:  "I wish I KNEW the answer."  (not "know")
⚠️ "As if / as though" takes the past:        "He acts as if he WERE the boss."
```

## Rule 10 — Common confusions ⭐⭐

| Pair | Distinction |
|---|---|
| affect / effect | affect = verb (to influence); effect = noun (result) — *effect* is also a verb meaning "to bring about" |
| accept / except | accept = receive; except = excluding |
| advice / advise | advice = noun; advise = verb |
| principal / principle | principal = head / main; principle = rule |
| stationary / stationery | stationary = not moving; stationery = paper goods (**e** for envelope) |
| complement / compliment | complement = completes; compliment = praise |
| its / it's | its = possessive; it's = it is |
| your / you're, their / there / they're | possessive vs contraction vs place |
| lose / loose | lose = misplace; loose = not tight |
| beside / besides | beside = next to; besides = in addition to |
| among / between | among = 3+; between = 2 (or distinct pairs) |
| fewer / less | fewer = countable; less = uncountable ⭐ *fewer people, less water* |
| much / many | many = countable; much = uncountable |
| amount / number | amount = uncountable; number = countable |
| farther / further | farther = physical distance; further = figurative/additional |
| lie / lay | lie (lay, lain) = recline, no object; lay (laid, laid) = put down, takes an object ⭐ |
| rise / raise | rise = go up (no object); raise = lift something (object) |
| adapt / adopt | adapt = adjust; adopt = take up |
| eminent / imminent | eminent = distinguished; imminent = about to happen |
| historic / historical | historic = significant; historical = relating to history |
| continual / continuous | continual = repeated; continuous = unbroken |
| assure / ensure / insure | assure a person; ensure a result; insure property |
| practice / practise | (British) practice = noun; practise = verb |

## Rule 11 — Redundancy ⭐

OAs love these because they look correct.
```
✗ return back        → return              ✗ repeat again      → repeat
✗ revert back        → revert              ✗ reason is because → reason is that ⭐
✗ each and every     → each / every        ✗ cousin brother    → cousin
✗ free gift          → gift                ✗ new innovation    → innovation
✗ advance planning   → planning            ✗ past history      → history
✗ end result         → result              ✗ close proximity   → proximity
✗ mutual cooperation → cooperation         ✗ ascend up         → ascend
✗ join together      → join                ✗ sufficient enough → sufficient
✗ more preferable    → preferable          ✗ absolutely essential → essential
```

## Rule 12 — Question tags, conjunctions and miscellany ⭐

```
QUESTION TAGS: positive statement → negative tag, and vice versa.
   "He is coming, ISN'T HE?"         "She can't swim, CAN SHE?"
   "Let's go, SHALL WE?"             "I am right, AREN'T I?"   ⭐ (irregular)
   "Open the door, WILL YOU?"

CONJUNCTION PAIRS — never double up: ⚠️
   ✗ "Although he was tired, BUT he continued."  →  drop "but"
   ✗ "Because he was late, SO he missed it."     →  drop "so"
   ✗ "No sooner did he arrive THAN..." ✓  but "No sooner … WHEN" ✗   ⭐
   ✓ "Hardly/Scarcely … WHEN";  "No sooner … THAN";  "Such … THAT";  "So … THAT"

INVERSION after negative openers ⭐
   "Hardly HAD he arrived when it started raining."   (not "he had")
   "Never HAVE I seen such a sight."
   "Not only DID he win, but he also broke the record."

"LEST" takes "should", never "not": ⭐
   ✓ "Work hard lest you SHOULD fail."   ✗ "lest you should not fail"
```

## Worked error-spotting examples ⭐

**1.** *(A) One of the students (B) who has topped (C) the examination (D) is my cousin. (E) No error*
```
Analysis: "who" refers to "the students" (plural), not to "one".
⇒ should be "who HAVE topped"
Answer: **(B)**
```

**2.** *(A) The manager along with (B) his team members (C) are going to attend (D) the conference.*
```
"along with" does not change the subject's number. Subject = "The manager" (singular).
⇒ "IS going to attend"
Answer: **(C)**
```

**3.** *(A) He is senior (B) than me (C) by three years (D) in this office.*
```
"senior" takes TO, not THAN. Also "me" after "to" is correct (object).
⇒ "senior TO me"
Answer: **(B)**
```

**4.** *(A) If I would have known (B) about the meeting (C) I would have (D) attended it.*
```
Third conditional: the if-clause takes "had + past participle", never "would have".
⇒ "If I HAD known"
Answer: **(A)**
```

**5.** *(A) The number of accidents (B) on this highway (C) have increased (D) considerably.*
```
"The number of" is SINGULAR (contrast with "A number of").
⇒ "HAS increased"
Answer: **(C)**
```

**6.** *(A) Each of the boys (B) were given (C) a prize (D) by the principal.*
```
"Each" is always singular.
⇒ "WAS given"
Answer: **(B)**
```

**7.** *(A) He discussed about (B) the problem (C) with his colleagues (D) at length.*
```
"Discuss" takes no preposition.
⇒ "He DISCUSSED the problem"
Answer: **(A)**
```

**8.** *(A) Neither the students (B) nor the teacher (C) were present (D) in the hall.*
```
With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the NEARER subject = "the teacher" (singular).
⇒ "WAS present"
Answer: **(C)**
```

## Practice set

Identify the error in each (or "no error"):

1. (A) The jury (B) have given (C) their verdict (D) unanimously.
2. (A) I prefer coffee (B) than tea (C) in the morning. (D) No error
3. (A) Walking along the beach, (B) the sunset (C) looked beautiful (D) to us.
4. (A) He is one of (B) the best player (C) in the team (D) this season.
5. (A) Unless you (B) do not work hard, (C) you will not (D) succeed.
6. (A) She has been (B) working here (C) since five years (D) now.
7. (A) The scenery (B) of Kashmir (C) are very (D) beautiful.
8. (A) Hardly had he (B) reached the station (C) than the train (D) departed.
9. (A) Everyone of the (B) participants (C) were given (D) a certificate.
10. (A) He lay the book (B) on the table (C) and left (D) the room.

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. **(E) No error** — "jury" as individuals acting separately takes a plural verb; this is
   acceptable. (If the answer key insists on the unit sense, it would be (B) "has given its".)
2. **(B)** — *prefer X TO Y*, never "than".
3. **(A)/(B)** — dangling modifier: the sunset was not walking. Correct: *Walking along the beach,
   we thought the sunset looked beautiful.* Standard keys mark **(B)**.
4. **(B)** — "one of the best **players**".
5. **(B)** — "Unless" already means "if not"; "do not" is a double negative. → *Unless you work hard*.
6. **(C)** — "since" takes a point in time; for a duration use **for five years**.
7. **(C)** — "scenery" is uncountable and singular → **is**.
8. **(C)** — *Hardly … WHEN*; *No sooner … THAN*.
9. **(C)** — "Everyone/Every one of" is singular → **was given**.
10. **(A)** — past of *lay* (to put) is **laid**: *He laid the book on the table.*
</details>

Before any verbal section, re-read in this order:
```
1. The subject-verb agreement table (Rule 1)          — 5 min
2. The preposition collocations (Rule 3) and the      — 4 min
   "no preposition" verb list
3. The redundancy list (Rule 11)                      — 2 min
4. Comparatives: senior/inferior/prefer + TO          — 1 min
5. Conditionals and the subjunctive (Rule 9)          — 3 min
```
