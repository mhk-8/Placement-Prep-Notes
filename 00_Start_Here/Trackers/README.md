# Trackers

Four files, each answering a different question. All four are **diagnostic instruments, not report cards** — the moment you curate them to look good, they stop telling you anything.

| File | Question it answers | Updated |
|---|---|---|
| `problem-tracker.md` | Which patterns are slow or fragile? What is due for re-solving? | Daily, at close-out |
| `topic-revision-tracker.md` | What concepts are due for recall today? What keeps slipping? | Daily, morning + close-out |
| `mock-scores.md` | Am I improving under time, and *how* am I failing? | After every mock |
| `application-tracker.md` | What is happening, and what is my next action on each company? | Within 2 h of any change |

## The rhythm

- **Every morning (10 min):** `topic-revision-tracker.md` → today's due queue → cold recall on paper.
- **Every evening (15 min, close-out):** log problems, log misses with a root-cause tag, schedule D+1/3/7/21 reviews, write tomorrow's first block.
- **Every Sunday (60 min):** weekly roll-ups in `problem-tracker.md` and `mock-scores.md`; rewrite the 14-day list in `application-tracker.md`; set next week's three targets in `study-plan.md` §10.
- **Every phase gate:** re-rate `syllabus-checklist.md`, fill the coverage tables, decide whether to advance or repeat a week.

## Why the root-cause tag matters more than the score

Every miss gets tagged `concept` / `bug` / `time` / `misread` / `panic`. Tally them in `mock-scores.md`. The tally tells you what to do next:

- Mostly **concept** → study more, targeted at the weak patterns.
- Mostly **bug** → you do not need more topics; you need a pre-submit checklist and a dry-run habit.
- Mostly **time** → enforce the caps and the read-all-questions-first rule; more mocks, not more theory.
- Mostly **misread** → restate every problem in your own words before coding.
- Mostly **panic** → exposure. More simulations under real conditions.

Four of those five are fixed by discipline, not by content. Most people default to "study more" for all five, which is why plateaus last for months.
