# Rules — Non-Negotiables

> Written once, in a calm moment, so that a tired or anxious version of you does not get to renegotiate them at 23:00.
> Revisit only at a phase gate. Between gates, these are settled.

---

## 1. Process rules

1. **Every wrong answer gets logged the same day**, with a root-cause tag. No exceptions, including on bad days — especially on bad days.
2. **Timer on for every problem.** Untimed practice does not count towards the day's target.
3. **35-minute cap on a medium.** At the cap, stop, read the editorial, log it, move on. Coming back to it tomorrow is allowed; grinding tonight is not.
4. **Write the attempt before reading the solution.** Even a wrong approach on paper.
5. **Notes in my own words, written while learning.** Copy-pasted notes are read zero times and remembered less.
6. **One problem list, one platform.** Switching lists is procrastination wearing a productive costume.
7. **Revision before new material, every day.** If only one happens, it is revision.
8. **A topic is "done" only when I can re-derive it cold three weeks later**, not when I have read it.

## 2. Commitment rules

9. **Primary OA language: ______** (fill this in during Week 0 and do not change it after 18 Oct). Changing language in November is how people lose offers.
10. **No new resource after 1 Nov.** No new course, playlist, book or list. Finish what is started.
11. **No new topics after 22 Nov.** Weeks 10 onward are reliability, not coverage.
12. **Resume frozen by 10 Oct.** Cosmetic edits allowed; content edits are not.

## 3. Boundary rules

13. **No technical study after 21:00.** Sleep is a performance input.
14. **One full evening off per week**, kept even in November.
15. **Phone in another room during deep blocks.** Not face-down on the desk.
16. **No comparing progress with peers.** Compare against the checklist, which is the only thing that is actually scored.
17. **No LeetCode/Reddit/Discord "what's the cutoff" doom-reading** in the 24 h before an OA. It changes nothing and costs composure.

## 4. Honesty rules

18. **Trackers are diagnostic instruments, not report cards.** The moment I curate them to look good, they become worthless.
19. **"Solved unaided" means unaided** — no hints, no peeking at tags, no glancing at the editorial's first line.
20. **A skipped day is logged as skipped.** Faked entries corrupt the spaced-repetition schedule downstream.
21. **In an interview, I say "I don't know" rather than bluff.** Bluffing is detected almost every time and costs more than the gap itself.

## 5. Recovery rules — for when the plan breaks

It will break. That is expected, and these rules are what make it survivable.

22. **A missed day is not a failed plan.** Resume at the next scheduled block; never try to "make up" two days in one — it produces a third bad day.
23. **If I fall more than 3 days behind:** do not restart the plan and do not compress it. Cut P3 topics, then P2, and keep the gates where they are.
24. **If I fail a phase gate:** repeat the last week of that phase rather than advancing on the calendar. Advancing on schedule alone is how people arrive at December with broad, shallow coverage.
25. **After a bad mock:** review it the same day, then take the evening off. Do not attempt a second mock to "fix" the score — that trains panic, not skill.
26. **After a rejection:** write the debrief within 24 h, extract the two concrete gaps, add them to the checklist, and stop replaying it. The next company does not know or care.

## 6. Escalation

27. **If I am stuck on a concept for more than two sessions**, I ask — a peer, a senior, a forum, or Claude — rather than spending a third session. Pride is not a study technique.
28. **If I am not sleeping or my mood is persistently flat for more than a week**, that is a real problem that outranks the plan. Talk to someone — the IITM wellness centre, a friend, family. A placement season is not worth your health, and functioning badly for six weeks will cost you more offers than any missed topic.
